The journey

Almost every Iceland itinerary sold in India stops at the Golden Circle and the south coast, which is like visiting India and stopping at Delhi. The north, the Diamond Circle of Dettifoss, Ásbyrgi canyon, Lake Mývatn's steaming earth and the whale bay at Húsavík, is where Iceland stops performing for tour buses and simply carries on being itself. We go there, because that is the difference between a photograph and a story.

The set pieces still get their due: Geysir erupting on schedule, Gullfoss thundering into its gorge, the black beach at Reynisfjara with the basalt columns and a firm briefing about the waves, they take people who do not listen, every year, and we say so plainly. Glacier lagoon at Jökulsárlón, where icebergs the size of houses drift to sea past seals that could not care less about you. Then north, where the crowds thin to nothing and Dettifoss, Europe's most powerful waterfall, announces itself as spray on the horizon before you hear it.

On northern-lights departures (September to March) we hunt them the honest way: aurora forecasts read nightly, the coach out when the sky earns it, and no promises, the lights are weather, not a show. This is the trip for travellers who have done Europe's cities and want the planet instead. A Schengen visa applies; book three months out, and we handle the paperwork checklist with you.

Day by day

Nine days, Reykjavík back to Reykjavík, saving the north for the days it's earned.

Days 3–4South CoastReynisfjara, Jökulsárlón glacier lagoon
Days 5–7The Diamond Circle & NorthDettifoss, Ásbyrgi, Mývatn, Akureyri
Days 8–9Back to Reykjavíkfarewell dinner, Blue Lagoon, departure
  • Day 1

    Arrive Keflavík, transfer to Reykjavík; evening walk to Hallgrímskirkja and the harbour. Nothing ambitious after a long flight, on purpose.

  • Day 2

    Golden Circle, Þingvellir's rift valley where two continents pull apart, Geysir, Gullfoss; hot-spring bathing at a quieter lagoon than the famous one.

  • Day 3

    The south coast, Seljalandsfoss (walk behind it), Skógafoss, Reynisfjara black beach with the briefing; night near Vík.

  • Day 4

    Vatnajökull country, Jökulsárlón glacier lagoon and the Diamond Beach; optional glacier walk with certified guides; night in the southeast.

  • Day 5

    The long, spectacular drive up the east fjords to the north; fishing villages, reindeer if the hills allow; night at Mývatn.

  • Day 6

    The Diamond Circle, Dettifoss, Ásbyrgi's horseshoe canyon, Húsavík; whale watching in season (sightings likely, never promised).

  • Day 7

    Mývatn's pseudocraters, the Námaskarð boiling earth, Goðafoss on the road west; night at Akureyri, the northern capital.

  • Day 8

    Fly or drive back to Reykjavík; farewell dinner; last aurora watch on winter departures.

  • Day 9

    Blue Lagoon en route to Keflavík for those who want the classic; depart.

Where you stay

Country hotels and guesthouses chosen for position on the ring, a farm stay under the glacier in the southeast, a lakeside property at Mývatn where the aurora can be watched from the door. Iceland's rural hotels are simple, spotless and warm; the luxury is outside the window.

Who it’s for

Couples, friends and photographers making the one big Arctic trip, and honeymooners who want the lights over a lagoon rather than a resort. The walking is short but the weather is a participant: wind, rain and ice are part of the deal, and days 5–7 involve real road hours. Not for travellers who need warmth, certainty or shopping, and not bookable late, the visa timeline and Iceland's small hotel stock both punish delay.
