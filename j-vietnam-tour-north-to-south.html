Vietnam · 7 nights · Gentle–Moderate

Vietnam North to South

Seven nights down the length of Vietnam, Hanoi, an overnight cruise on Ha Long Bay, lantern-lit Hoi An and Ho Chi Minh City, on the easiest visa in Asia.

The journey

Most Vietnam itineraries sold in India are a north-only or south-only compromise, because doing the full length "doesn't fit in a week". It fits fine, Vietnam has cheap, reliable internal flights, and two of them turn a compromise into the whole country. What does not fit is rushing Ha Long Bay as a day trip from Hanoi, which is eight hours of bus for two hours of boat. We sleep on the bay instead.

The overnight cruise is the spine of the trip: kayaking into limestone grottoes in the late afternoon, squid fishing off the back deck after dinner, and waking up anchored among karsts with nobody else in sight. Then Hoi An, which we give two nights because one is a crime, a tailor-made shirt takes 24 hours, and the old town only becomes itself after the day-trippers leave and the lanterns come on. We end in Ho Chi Minh City with the Cu Chi tunnels and a street food evening led by someone who eats there, not performs there.

The visa is the quiet selling point: Vietnam's e-visa for Indians is filled in online in twenty minutes and typically approved within days. Combined with the price of a very good meal being ₹300, this is the trip we recommend most often to families taking their first international holiday and to couples who want maximum country per rupee.

Day by day

  • Day 1

    Arrive Hanoi, transfer, evening cyclo ride through the Old Quarter and egg coffee at a pavement café. Easy by design.

  • Day 2

    Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum complex, Temple of Literature, Train Street mid-morning, water puppet theatre in the evening.

  • Day 3

    Drive to Ha Long Bay (2.5 hours), board the overnight cruise by noon. Kayaking, cave visit, sunset on deck, squid fishing after dinner.

  • Day 4

    Sunrise tai chi on deck for the willing, brunch cruising back, fly Hanoi to Da Nang, transfer to Hoi An. First lantern walk after dark.

  • Day 5

    Hoi An, old town on foot in the morning (Japanese Bridge, merchant houses), tailoring measurements, afternoon free for the beach or a basket-boat ride in the coconut palms.

  • Day 6

    Hoi An, morning cooking class in a village kitchen, afternoon collection of tailoring, evening free among the lanterns.

  • Day 7

    Fly to Ho Chi Minh City. Cu Chi tunnels in the afternoon, street food walk in District 4 at night.

  • Day 8

    War Remnants Museum and Ben Thanh Market in the morning, depart.

Where you stay

Central, characterful hotels, Old Quarter in Hanoi so you step out into the noise, and within walking distance of the old town in Hoi An so the lanterns are yours after dinner. The cruise boat is a mid-size vessel with proper en-suite cabins, chosen after trying the giant party boats and deciding never again.

Who it’s for

Families with children of almost any age, couples, and first-time international travellers, the pace is gentle, the food is friendly, and nothing on the itinerary is physically demanding beyond the optional kayaking. Not for travellers who want one resort and a pool; you change cities four times, and that is the point.
