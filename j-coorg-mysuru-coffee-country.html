The journey

The standard mistake with Mysuru is treating it as a lunch stop on the way to Coorg. The palace deserves better, specifically, it deserves a Sunday or festival evening, when nearly a hundred thousand bulbs go on at once and the whole building turns to lit filigree. We plan around the illumination calendar wherever the dates allow, and we do Devaraja Market in the morning, when the jasmine sellers are stringing flowers by the metre and the banana merchants have opinions about everything.

Coorg is where the journey slows down on purpose. Three nights on and around working coffee estates, walking the plantation with someone who grew up on it, learning why the blossom week in March is the best-smelling seven days in India, tasting Coorgi pandi curry and bamboo-shoot dishes that never left the district. Dubare elephant camp is done the right way: watching and, where permitted, helping with the river routine, rather than riding, a distinction we hold to.

This is a journey for families and for anyone whose ideal holiday involves a verandah, rain-washed hills, and a second cup of estate coffee going cold because the conversation was good. Grandparents manage it easily; children have elephants, waterfalls and estate space to run in.

Day by day

Six days, start to finish. The route, then the rhythm of it.

Days 1–2Mysurupalace and market
Days 3–5Coorgcoffee estate, Dubare, waterfalls
Day 6Departureonward via Mysuru or Mangaluru
  • Day 1

    Arrive Mysuru; evening at the palace for the illumination if the calendar allows, else a gentle city drive. Easy on purpose.

  • Day 2

    Devaraja Market early with the flower sellers; Mysore Palace interiors; afternoon at Chamundi Hill; silk or sandalwood stop for those who want it.

  • Day 3

    Drive to Coorg via Bylakuppe's golden Namdroling Monastery, a Tibetan world you did not expect in Karnataka; reach the estate by evening.

  • Day 4

    Morning plantation walk, coffee, pepper vines, cardamom; afternoon at Abbey Falls and Raja's Seat for sunset.

  • Day 5

    Dubare elephant camp on the Cauvery in the morning; afternoon free on the estate, verandah, book, rain if you are lucky; farewell Coorgi dinner.

  • Day 6

    Unhurried departure; drive to Mysuru or Mangaluru side for onward travel.

Where you stay

A comfortable heritage-side hotel in Mysuru and estate stays in Coorg we have used for years, planter's bungalows and estate homestays where dinner is cooked in the family kitchen and the silence at night is total.

Who it’s for

Families across three generations, the walking is optional, the elephants are compulsory joy, and estates absorb children happily. Also couples who want green hills without a trek. Not for travellers who need urban energy or nightlife; Coorg's evenings are firesides and early dinners, and the nearest anything is a winding hour away.
