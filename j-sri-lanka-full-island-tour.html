Sri Lanka: The Full Island

Seven nights from Sigiriya's rock fortress through Kandy and the hill-country train to Ella, ending on the ramparts of Galle, the whole island, on a visa that costs Indians nothing.

The journey

Sri Lanka gets sold to Indians as a beach add-on, which is like selling Rajasthan as a hotel pool. The island packs an eighth-century rock citadel, a sacred tooth relic, tea country that out-greens Munnar, a train ride people fly across the world for, and a Dutch fort town, all within seven hours' drive of each other. The mistake most itineraries make is beach-first; the beach means more when you have earned it coming down from the hills.

Two set pieces anchor the week. First, Sigiriya climbed at seven in the morning, before the heat and the crowds, when the frescoes are in shade and the summit, an entire royal palace footprint two hundred metres in the sky, is nearly empty. Second, the train from Kandy through the tea country toward Ella: we book reserved seats and we tell you honestly that the famous "doors open, feet hanging out" photographs come from the unreserved carriages, which we do not recommend with luggage. The view is identical from a seat.

For Indian travellers the practicalities are as friendly as they get, the visa is free for Indians, the flight from most metros is shorter than Delhi–Chennai, and the food (hoppers, kottu, crab curry) feels like a cousin's kitchen. One honest note on season: December to March is the west and south coast's dry season, which is when this routing shines; May to September favours the east coast instead, and we will say so rather than sell you Galle in the rain.

Day by day

  • Day 1

    Arrive Colombo, drive to the Sigiriya area (4 hours with a lunch stop), evening village walk and an early night before the climb.

  • Day 2

    Sigiriya rock at 7 a.m., up and down before the heat. Afternoon at the Dambulla cave temples, five caves of Buddhas in lamplight.

  • Day 3

    Drive to Kandy via a spice garden stop. Temple of the Tooth at evening puja, drums echoing off the lake.

  • Day 4

    Kandy, Peradeniya botanical gardens in the morning, then the hill train toward Ella (reserved seats), tea gardens unspooling for four hours. Evening in Ella's one cheerful street.

  • Day 5

    Ella, Little Adam's Peak at sunrise (45 minutes, gentle), Nine Arch Bridge as the train crosses, tea factory visit with a proper tasting.

  • Day 6

    Drive down to Galle (4 hours), afternoon walking the fort ramparts at sunset, lighthouse, Dutch churches, cricket on the green below.

  • Day 7

    Beach morning at Unawatuna or a whale-watching option in season (Nov–Apr, Mirissa), farewell dinner inside the fort.

  • Day 8

    Drive to Colombo airport (2.5 hours via the expressway), depart.

Where you stay

Small hotels with verandas and gardens rather than tower blocks, paddy views near Sigiriya, a hillside perch in Ella where breakfast comes with a valley, and a boutique property inside or beside the Galle fort walls so the ramparts are your evening walk.

Who it’s for

Families and couples who want variety without hard effort, the only real exertion is the Sigiriya climb (1,200 steps, taken slowly, with rests) and the gentle Little Adam's Peak walk. Not ideal for travellers with serious knee trouble unless they are content to skip Sigiriya's summit; the water gardens at its base are worth the visit alone.
