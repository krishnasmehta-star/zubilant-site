Georgia & Azerbaijan: The Caucasus Road

Seven nights overland from Baku's flame towers to the Kazbegi mountains via Sheki and Tbilisi, two countries, one road trip, and an e-visa you can get from your sofa in three days.

The journey

Everyone flies into Tbilisi, does two wine tastings, and flies home calling it the Caucasus. The Caucasus is a road, not a city, and the honest way to drive it is west from Baku, watching the landscape turn from Caspian desert to walnut orchards to proper snow-topped mountains over four days. Both countries issue Indian passport holders e-visas online, approved in days, no embassy queue, no bank-statement theatre. That is half the reason this route is filling up with Indian travellers before the big operators have noticed.

Baku surprises people: a walled medieval old city sitting under buildings shaped like flames, mud volcanoes an hour out, and rock carvings at Gobustan older than most civilisations you have heard of. Then the road, a night in Sheki at the old caravanserai town with its stained-glass palace, the land border crossed on foot with our escort handling both sides, and into Georgia's wine country before Tbilisi's sulphur baths and balconied lanes. The last leg climbs the Georgian Military Highway to Kazbegi, where the Gergeti Trinity Church stands against a 5,000-metre peak and the khinkali arrive twelve to a plate.

This lands hardest for groups of friends and younger couples who want a trip that photographs like Europe, costs like Southeast Asia, and still feels slightly earned. It is trending for 2026 and the prices will not stay this reasonable.

Day by day

  • Day 1

    Arrive Baku, transfer, evening walk along the Caspian boulevard with the Flame Towers lit, nothing scheduled, deliberately.

  • Day 2

    Baku old city on foot in the morning, Gobustan rock art and mud volcanoes in the afternoon; tea the Azeri way, with jam.

  • Day 3

    Drive northwest to Sheki (about 5 hours with stops); the Khan's Palace stained glass before closing, night in the caravanserai quarter.

  • Day 4

    Cross the land border into Georgia on foot, escort handles both sides; wine-country lunch in Kakheti, evening arrival in Tbilisi.

  • Day 5

    Tbilisi day, old town balconies, the Narikala cable car, sulphur bath for the willing, wine bars for the rest.

  • Day 6

    Georgian Military Highway to Kazbegi, Ananuri fortress and the Russia–Georgia friendship viewpoint en route; 4x4 up to Gergeti Trinity Church in the late afternoon light.

  • Day 7

    Morning in the mountains, drive back to Tbilisi; farewell supra dinner with more toasts than strictly necessary.

  • Day 8

    Transfer to Tbilisi airport, fly home.

Where you stay

Boutique hotels in Baku's old city and Tbilisi's balconied lanes, small, characterful places we have used since this route was empty. In Sheki, a guesthouse in the caravanserai quarter; in Kazbegi, a mountain lodge where the peak fills the breakfast-room window. Character over stars, everywhere.

Who it’s for

Groups of friends, couples, and confident travellers in their twenties to fifties who like a road trip with texture. There is one long drive day and one foot border crossing, and mountain-town plumbing is mountain-town plumbing. Not for travellers who need five-star consistency every night, and not ideal for those with limited mobility, old towns here mean cobbles and slopes.
