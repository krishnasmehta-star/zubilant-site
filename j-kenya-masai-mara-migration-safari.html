Kenya: The Masai Mara Migration

Six nights from Nairobi deep into the Masai Mara for the July-to-October migration window, closing at Lake Naivasha, the once-in-a-lifetime trip, planned so it actually delivers once in a lifetime.

The journey

The single most common safari mistake is arithmetic: people buy a "Kenya highlights" tour that gives the Masai Mara two nights out of eight, then spend the other six in transit between parks that are each a lesser Mara. During the migration months, the Mara is where a million and a half wildebeest are, and we respond to that fact with three full nights inside or beside the reserve, which in practice means five game drives instead of two, and five chances at a river crossing instead of a coin flip.

We are straight with you about the crossings, because operators who promise them are lying: the wildebeest cross the Mara river when they cross it, sometimes twice in a morning, sometimes not for days. What three nights buys is probability and patience, your driver-guide parked quietly at a likely bend with breakfast boxes, rather than racing back for a hotel lunch. The rest of the cast keeps office hours regardless: lion prides at dawn, elephant families in the Musiara marsh, and a sky that goes further than any you have stood under. We finish at Lake Naivasha, a softer landscape of boat rides among hippos and a walk, actually on foot, among giraffe and zebra on Crescent Island, which lands especially well with children after days confined to the vehicle.

Practicalities for Indian passport holders: Kenya requires an online eTA, approved in a few days, and yellow fever vaccination is required for entry, book the jab at least ten days before flying, and we remind you at booking because it is the one item that cannot be fixed at the airport. This is the most expensive journey in our list and the one nobody has ever told us was overpriced afterwards.

Day by day

  • Day 1

    Arrive Nairobi, transfer to the hotel, afternoon at the Sheldrick elephant orphanage and the Giraffe Centre. Early night; the distances start tomorrow.

  • Day 2

    Drive to the Masai Mara (5–6 hours, Rift Valley viewpoint stop), arrive for lunch, first evening game drive as the light goes gold.

  • Day 3

    Full Mara day, dawn drive with breakfast boxes at a river bend if crossings look likely, midday rest at camp, late-afternoon drive for cats on the move.

  • Day 4

    Full Mara day, dawn-to-noon drive across the Musiara and Paradise plains, optional balloon safari at sunrise (booked ahead), evening visit to a Maasai village that has hosted our groups for years.

  • Day 5

    Last dawn drive, then to Lake Naivasha (4–5 hours). Evening boat ride among hippos and fish eagles.

  • Day 6

    Morning walk with giraffe and zebra on Crescent Island, no predators, so on foot is safe, then return to Nairobi. Farewell dinner.

  • Day 7

    Depart Nairobi.

Where you stay

In the Mara, a tented camp, and "tented" here means a proper bed, an attached bathroom with hot water, and canvas walls through which you will hear lions at 3 a.m., which is the point. In Nairobi and Naivasha, comfortable garden hotels we have used for years. Vegetarian and Jain meals are handled well with notice; safari kitchens are more flexible than their remoteness suggests.

Who it’s for

Multi-generational families marking an occasion, couples, and anyone for whom the migration has sat on the list for a decade, no fitness required beyond climbing into a Land Cruiser, but game-drive days are long (bumpy roads, 5 a.m. starts) and rewarding in direct proportion. Not for very young children, most camps set minimum ages, and a six-hour drive defeats a four-year-old, and not for travellers who cannot forgive weather and wildlife for keeping their own schedule.
