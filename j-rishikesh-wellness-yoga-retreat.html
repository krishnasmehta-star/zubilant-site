Rishikesh & the Ganga Foothills: A Wellness Week

Six nights of yoga, river and quiet in and above Rishikesh in the cool October-to-March season, a wellness week built around one good ashram-style routine, not a spa menu.

The journey

Rishikesh has a split personality, and most wellness packages book you into the wrong half. The backpacker bridge circus around Laxman Jhula is not where you go to slow down. The town's real gift sits upriver and uphill, quiet stretches of the Ganga where the morning practice happens to the sound of water, not traffic.

Our week runs on a simple rhythm we refuse to overload: yoga and pranayama in the morning, one unhurried thing in the day, a forest walk to a waterfall, a cooking session, a talk with a teacher who has spent decades here, and the river in the evening. We attend the Ganga aarti once at Parmarth Niketan with the crowds, because it is worth seeing, and once from a ghat almost nobody uses, because that is worth more. No itinerary tetris. The gaps are the point.

It lands hardest for people arriving tired, from work, from caregiving, from a hard year. By day three the phone-checking slows down. By day five most guests have stopped asking what is next. That is the product.

Day by day

  • Day 1

    Arrive Rishikesh by afternoon; gentle orientation walk along the river, early dinner. No yoga on day one, deliberately.

  • Day 2

    Morning practice; afternoon walk to a quiet Ganga beach upstream; evening aarti at Parmarth Niketan.

  • Day 3

    Morning practice; late-morning forest walk toward Neer waterfall; free afternoon; restorative session before dinner.

  • Day 4

    Morning practice; sattvic cooking session at midday; afternoon fully free; sunset from a near-empty ghat.

  • Day 5

    Morning practice; day loop to Vashishta Gufa cave and the riverbank at Shivpuri; evening satsang or free.

  • Day 6

    Morning practice; Beatles Ashram in the late morning before it fills; farewell dinner by the river.

  • Day 7

    Final short practice for early risers; depart for Dehradun airport or the train at Haridwar.

Where you stay

A quiet riverside retreat above the noise of the bridges, ashram discipline in the schedule, hotel comfort in the rooms. It is a property we have used for years because the kitchen is genuinely sattvic, the yoga hall faces the water, and the management understands that silence after 10 pm is a feature.

Who it’s for

Adults who want a genuine reset, solo travellers, couples, friends, and seniors, who manage this pace comfortably on level riverside paths. Honestly not for children; the routine, the quiet hours and the sattvic kitchen are built for grown-ups, and a bored ten-year-old will unbuild them for everyone.
