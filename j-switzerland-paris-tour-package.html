Switzerland & Paris: Europe for First-Timers

Eight nights from Interlaken to Lucerne to Paris by rail, the Jungfrau, the lakes and the Eiffel Tower without a single airport in between, on the trains the Swiss themselves use.

The journey

The mistake most first-time Europe itineraries make is trying to buy six countries with one Schengen visa. Six countries in ten days is six hotel check-ins, four early-morning coaches, and a memory of motorway service stations. We do two countries and do them by train, because in Switzerland the train is not transport, it is the sightseeing. The Interlaken–Lucerne run over the Brünig Pass is worth more than most guided city tours we have seen.

Three nights in Interlaken puts the Jungfrau day within easy reach: the railway up through the Eiger to Europe's highest station, snow underfoot in July if you want it. Then Lucerne, the wooden Chapel Bridge, a slow boat on the lake, and an evening when the escort points you to the old town and lets you get pleasantly lost. Then the TGV to Paris at 300 km/h, and three nights for the Louvre at a humane pace, Montmartre in the morning before the crowds, and the Eiffel Tower at night when it sparkles on the hour.

One thing we say plainly: the Schengen visa takes time. Appointment slots in India run four to eight weeks out in high season, so this journey needs booking at least three months ahead. We handle the documentation checklist and the itinerary letters; the biometric appointment is the one thing only you can attend. Couples on a honeymoon and families with teenagers get the most from this route.

Day by day

  • Day 1

    Arrive Zurich, rail to Interlaken along Lake Zurich and the Brünig line; evening walk on the Höheweg with both mountains and both lakes in view. Easy, on purpose.

  • Day 2

    Jungfraujoch day, cogwheel rail through Kleine Scheidegg to the Top of Europe; snow, the Sphinx terrace, and back down by mid-afternoon before the altitude tires anyone.

  • Day 3

    Lauterbrunnen valley morning (waterfalls, Trümmelbach), afternoon free for paragliding, chocolate, or nothing at all.

  • Day 4

    Golden-pass rail to Lucerne; Chapel Bridge and old town on foot before dinner.

  • Day 5

    Lake Lucerne boat in the morning, Mount Pilatus or Titlis excursion in the afternoon; fondue dinner.

  • Day 6

    TGV Lyria to Paris (about 4.5 hours); evening Seine river cruise as the city lights up.

  • Day 7

    Louvre with timed entry in the morning, we do three hours and the highlights, not eight and exhaustion; afternoon Champs-Élysées and Arc de Triomphe; Eiffel Tower sparkle at night.

  • Day 8

    Montmartre and Sacré-Cœur in the morning quiet, afternoon free, Versailles optional for the energetic; farewell dinner.

  • Day 9

    Transfer to Charles de Gaulle, fly home.

Where you stay

Family-run three- and four-star hotels within walking distance of the station in Interlaken and Lucerne, in Switzerland, near-the-station is the luxury. In Paris, a hotel in a real neighbourhood with a metro stop on the corner, not a tower by the ring road. Properties we have used for years, all with breakfast worth waking for.

Who it’s for

First-time Europe travellers, honeymooners, and families with children old enough to enjoy a museum, roughly ten and up. It involves real walking: cobbles, stations, two to five kilometres a day. Not for travellers who want a coach door-to-door at every sight, and not bookable on short notice, the visa timeline is the visa timeline, and we will not pretend otherwise.
