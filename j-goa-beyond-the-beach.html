Goa Beyond the Beach

Five nights of the Goa most visitors never find, the Latin quarter of Fontainhas, spice farms, ferry-only Divar island and the old churches, with beach afternoons kept sacred.

The journey

Goa has spent forty years being sold as a beach with a state attached. The beach is real and we will not pretend otherwise, but the state attached to it is four hundred and fifty years of Indo-Portuguese life, and most visitors leave without seeing an hour of it. Fontainhas at eight in the morning, when the ochre and indigo houses catch the light and the bakers' bicycles are out with fresh poi, is a different country from the Goa of the brochures.

Our structure is simple and we defend it: mornings for the real Goa, afternoons for the beach. So, the Basilica of Bom Jesus and Sé Cathedral before the coaches arrive; the ferry to Divar island, where there are no resorts, no shacks, and village Goa continues as if the twentieth century were optional; a working spice farm with lunch cooked over wood fire; a river cruise at sunset. Every afternoon, without exception, you are free on the sand. We plan nothing after two o'clock, and guests thank us for it by day three.

This lands hardest for couples and families who suspect there is more to Goa than they have been shown, and who want both halves without choosing. If your ideal Goa is entirely horizontal, book a resort and save the difference, this journey assumes curiosity before noon.

Day by day

  • Day 1

    Arrive Goa; check in near the beach; sunset walk on the sand, dinner of the first fish curry. Easy on purpose.

  • Day 2

    Old Goa morning, Bom Jesus and Sé Cathedral early; Fontainhas walk with a pastry stop; beach afternoon.

  • Day 3

    Ferry to Divar island, cycles or a slow drive through the villages, lunch in a Goan home-style kitchen; beach afternoon.

  • Day 4

    Spice farm morning inland with a wood-fire lunch; beach afternoon; evening sunset cruise on the Mandovi.

  • Day 5

    Free morning, or the Saturday market if the calendar obliges; final beach afternoon; farewell dinner at a beach shack we have trusted for years.

  • Day 6

    Departures.

Where you stay

One well-chosen property for all five nights, close enough to the beach that the afternoon promise is kept without a drive. A comfortable, unflashy hotel we have used for years, the kind where the staff remember how you take your tea by day two.

Who it’s for

Couples and families who want Goa with its history attached, and travellers on a second or third visit ready to go deeper. November to February is the honest window, dry, bright, warm sea. Not for nightlife-first travellers; our mornings start early and the itinerary makes no provision for recovering from them.
