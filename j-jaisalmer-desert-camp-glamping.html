Thar Under Canvas: Desert Rajasthan Glamping

Four nights split between Jaisalmer's golden city and a luxury desert camp deep in the Thar, proper beds under canvas, dune sunsets without the day-tripper circus, November to February only.

The journey

The desert most tourists see is Sam at sunset: a hundred jeeps, camel touts, loudspeakers, and a queue for the dune. That is not the Thar. The Thar is what happens twenty minutes past the last parked vehicle, silence with a texture to it, sand going violet after the sun drops, and a night sky that makes city children ask genuine questions.

So we split the trip cleanly. Two nights in Jaisalmer for the living fort, one of the last inhabited citadels anywhere, best walked at eight in the morning when the lanes belong to residents, and the havelis whose stone carving nobody has matched since. Then two nights under canvas at a camp away from the Sam scrum, with real beds, attached bathrooms, and dinners around a fire with folk musicians who play because it is Tuesday, not because a tour bus arrived. The camel ride happens at the hour the light is right, and it is optional; the dune at sunrise with tea is not, and nobody has ever asked for a refund on it.

Couples book this for the obvious reasons. Families book it because a night under canvas in the dunes does something to children that no resort pool can, and in the November–February window, the desert is cold-morning, warm-day perfect.

Day by day

  • Day 1

    Arrive Jaisalmer by afternoon; sunset from the rooftops facing the fort, dinner in the old town. Gentle start.

  • Day 2

    The fort at eight before the lanes fill, then Patwon ki Haveli; free afternoon; evening at Gadisar Lake as the light goes gold.

  • Day 3

    Slow morning; drive into the desert (1.5–2 hours); settle into camp, camel or jeep to the dunes for sunset, dinner and folk music by the fire.

  • Day 4

    Sunrise tea on the dunes for early risers; the day at desert pace, a village walk, a long lunch, an unashamed afternoon nap under canvas; final night sky.

  • Day 5

    Unhurried breakfast; return to Jaisalmer for flights and trains.

Where you stay

In Jaisalmer, a haveli-style hotel in golden stone within sight of the fort. In the desert, a tented camp we have used for years, canvas outside, proper beds, rugs and attached bathrooms with hot water inside, far enough from Sam that the loudest thing at night is the wind. Winter desert nights drop near freezing; the camp does hot-water bottles, and so should your packing.

Who it’s for

Couples, including honeymooners, and families who want the desert done properly. Seniors manage it comfortably; the pace is gentle, though the fort's lanes are cobbled and camp paths are sand. Not for travellers who need air-conditioned corridors between themselves and the outdoors, and not for June, we simply do not run this outside the cool months.
