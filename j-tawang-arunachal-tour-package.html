Tawang & the High Monasteries of Arunachal

Eight nights on the great road from Guwahati through Dirang to Tawang and its 17th-century monastery, over the 4,170-metre Sela Pass and back, in the clear windows of March–April and October–November.

The journey

Tawang is not a destination you visit; it is a road you earn. The itineraries that go wrong here all make the same error, they treat the two-day climb from the Assam plains as transit and try to compress it, which is how travellers arrive at 3,000 metres headachy, exhausted, and blaming Tawang for it. The road through the Kameng gorge, the apple orchards of Dirang, and the prayer-flagged crest of Sela Pass is half the journey. We schedule it like it matters, because it does.

So we climb in stages: a night at Bhalukpong where the plains end, two nights in Dirang at a sensible 1,600 metres, with the Sangti valley and a 500-year-old dzong most vehicles drive straight past, and only then over Sela to Tawang. Three nights there means the monastery, home to several hundred monks above a town that still moves to its bells, gets visited twice: once with the crowds at midday, and once at morning prayers, which is the version you will describe to people for years. The war memorial and Jaswant Garh get told properly, not pointed at through a window.

This is a journey for friends and road-trip veterans who measure a trip by what it asked of them. Permits, army checkposts, and the mountain-road logistics are our problem; the passenger seat over Sela in October, with the first snow on the peaks and prayer flags snapping, is yours.

Day by day

  • Day 1

    Arrive Guwahati; Kamakhya temple if timing allows, permits check, briefing over dinner. Easy on purpose.

  • Day 2

    Drive to Bhalukpong (5 hours) where Assam's tea flats hit the Arunachal wall; riverside evening on the Kameng.

  • Day 3

    The gorge road to Dirang (6 hours), climbing through Sessa and Nechiphu; evening walk in Dirang bazaar.

  • Day 4

    Dirang day, Sangti valley in the morning, the old dzong and hot springs after lunch; a deliberate acclimatisation day at 1,600 m.

  • Day 5

    Over Sela Pass (4,170 m), prayer flags, the lake, tea at the top; Jaswant Garh memorial told properly; descend to Tawang (7–8 hours total).

  • Day 6

    Tawang Monastery at morning prayers, the museum and the giant Buddha; afternoon at the war memorial; local market before dinner.

  • Day 7

    Day trip toward PT Tso and Bum La road lakes (army permits and weather permitting); else Urgelling monastery, birthplace of the sixth Dalai Lama.

  • Day 8

    The long return over Sela to Dirang (7–8 hours); farewell dinner with the group comparing photographs.

  • Day 9

    Descend to Guwahati (9–10 hours) for late flights, or overnight Tezpur option for morning departures.

Where you stay

The best available on a frontier road, chosen for warmth and kitchens rather than lobbies, a riverside property at Bhalukpong, a family-run hotel in Dirang we have used for years, and a solid, honestly heated hotel in Tawang town near the market. Power and hot water are reliable; room service is not a concept here, and the trip is better for it.

Who it’s for

Friends' groups, road-trippers and travellers who want the least-visited corner of the Eastern Himalaya with the logistics handled. Not for anyone who struggles with long winding drives, three days exceed six hours, and not for travellers with cardiac or respiratory conditions, given the hours spent above 4,000 metres at Sela. Children under twelve and the mountain roads of Arunachal are, in our experience, a poor pairing.
