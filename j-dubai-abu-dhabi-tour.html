The journey

Dubai is the easiest international holiday an Indian family can take, and most tours repay that convenience by showing you the inside of three malls and a fountain. We think the Emirates deserve better. Old Dubai still exists: abra boats crossing the creek for one dirham, the gold and spice souks running on haggling and cardamom, the restored Al Fahidi quarter where the city fits into wind-tower houses. That is our first morning, before a single escalator.

The two set pieces most itineraries shortchange get full evenings. The desert is done properly, a conservation-reserve camp rather than a dune-bashing conveyor belt, camels at sunset, falconry, dinner under actual stars. And Abu Dhabi gets a full day, because the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque and the Louvre Abu Dhabi, Jean Nouvel's dome scattering light like a palm canopy over eight thousand years of human making, are jointly worth more than any observation deck. We do the Burj Khalifa too, at sunset, timed so you see the city by day and by light. We are not ascetics.

This is the trip we recommend for three-generation families: November to March weather is genuinely lovely, every distance is short, wheelchairs and pushchairs work everywhere, and the food runs from Kerala thali to high tea without a wrong step. The UAE visa for Indians is a straightforward online process measured in days, and dual-city ambition costs nothing extra in effort, Abu Dhabi is ninety minutes down a very smooth road.

  • Day 1

    Arrive Dubai, transfer, evening at the Dubai Fountain and an outside table at Souk Al Bahar. Gentle start.

  • Day 2

    Old Dubai morning, Al Fahidi quarter, abra across the creek, spice and gold souks with haggling coached. Afternoon rest, Burj Khalifa at sunset.

  • Day 3

    Morning free (Dubai Mall aquarium or the beach at Kite Beach), afternoon into the desert reserve, camels, falconry, sunset on the dunes, barbecue dinner under the stars.

  • Day 4

    Full day Abu Dhabi, Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque in the morning light, Louvre Abu Dhabi after lunch, corniche drive, back to Dubai by evening.

  • Day 5

    Free day, Miracle Garden (Nov–Apr) and Global Village for families, or Jumeirah beach; optional Dubai Frame. Farewell dinner on a creek dhow.

Where you stay

One well-located four-star hotel for all five nights, near a Metro station and the Dubai Mall district, so free time is genuinely free. Staying put the whole week is a deliberate choice families thank us for by day three.

Who it’s for

Three-generation families, first-time international travellers, and anyone celebrating something, the pace is gentle, distances short, and every venue works for wheelchairs and pushchairs (the desert camp included, with notice). Not for travellers chasing culture-shock or wilderness; the Emirates are polished by design, and this itinerary embraces that rather than apologising for it.
