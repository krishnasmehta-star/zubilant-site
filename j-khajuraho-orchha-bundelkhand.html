Khajuraho, Orchha & the Bundelkhand Road

Five nights through Bundelkhand's great stone country, the Khajuraho temples read properly, Orchha's river-side cenotaphs at sunset, and roads most tourists never take.

The journey

Khajuraho has a reputation problem: everyone arrives for one thing, giggles through the Western Group in ninety minutes, and leaves. What gets missed is that these are among the finest sculpted buildings human beings have ever raised, a thousand years old, carved with a confidence in daily life, war, music and yes, love, that no other site in India matches. We give the temples two visits with a guide who reads the stone panel by panel, and the second morning, when you return knowing what you are looking at, is when the place opens up.

Orchha is the reason this journey exists as five nights and not three. A near-abandoned royal capital on the Betwa river, its palaces and chhatris, the riverside cenotaphs of the Bundela kings, sit almost empty of visitors. We take the evening aarti at the Ram Raja temple, the only place in India where Ram is worshipped as a king, and we let you sit on the river rocks at sunset while vultures wheel over the cenotaph spires. It is one of the quietest grand places left in the country.

The Bundelkhand road itself is the connective tissue, small fort towns, dhaba lunches, fields of mustard in season. This journey lands hardest for travellers who have done the Golden Triangle and want the India that existed before the tour buses were invented.

Day by day

  • Day 1

    Arrive Khajuraho by flight or via Jhansi; evening light-and-sound show at the temples as an overture. Easy on purpose.

  • Day 2

    Western Group of temples in the morning with our guide, panel by panel; afternoon rest; Eastern and Jain temples at golden hour.

  • Day 3

    Second, quieter dawn visit for those who want it; drive the Bundelkhand road to Orchha with a proper dhaba lunch stop; first view of the cenotaphs from the bridge.

  • Day 4

    Orchha in full, Jahangir Mahal and Raja Mahal in the morning, the sanctuary walk or river time in the afternoon, evening aarti at Ram Raja temple.

  • Day 5

    Sunrise at the chhatris on the Betwa; unhurried day, optional gentle river rafting or simply the town's lanes; farewell dinner.

  • Day 6

    Transfer to Jhansi for onward trains, or back to Khajuraho for flights.

Where you stay

A comfortable garden hotel near the temple zone in Khajuraho and a heritage-style property in Orchha within walking distance of the palaces, properties we have used for years, chosen because in towns this small, location decides whether you see the monuments at their empty hours or with everyone else.

Who it’s for

Travellers who love history and can happily spend two hours reading a single building; couples; photographers. The pace is gentle and drives are short. Not for children under about ten, the temples reward patience they do not yet have, and not for anyone expecting resort amenities; Bundelkhand's hotels are comfortable, not lavish.
