The journey

Most Indian families do Thailand as Bangkok-plus-Pattaya, which means two days of malls and a beach town built for a different kind of tourist. The country's best family fortnight sits ninety minutes west of the capital, on the River Kwai, and almost nobody routes through it. Thailand is also visa-free for Indian passport holders now, which quietly removes the single biggest reason families used to postpone this trip.

We do Bangkok properly but briefly, the Grand Palace at opening time before the tour buses, a longtail boat through the Thonburi canals where the city still lives on water, dinner at a riverside table rather than a rooftop queue. Then we go to Kanchanaburi: the bridge over the River Kwai, a ride on the surviving stretch of the Death Railway with the cliff dropping to the river on one side, and a night in a resort where the river runs past your balcony. The children remember the train; the grandparents remember what the railway cost to build. Then three nights on the Gulf coast with nothing scheduled except a snorkelling morning and the escort on hand if you want more.

This lands hardest for families with school-age children in the winter break. November to March is Thailand's cool, dry season, the one window when Bangkok's pavements are actually walkable at noon.

  • Day 1

    Arrive Bangkok, private transfer, evening at leisure, a gentle first night, on purpose; short walk to the riverside for dinner.

  • Day 2

    Grand Palace and Wat Pho at opening time, longtail canal boat in the late morning, afternoon free for Chatuchak or the pool.

  • Day 3

    Early drive to Damnoen Saduak floating market, on to Kanchanaburi; the bridge over the River Kwai at golden hour; night by the river.

  • Day 4

    Morning ride on the Death Railway's surviving stretch, war cemetery for those who wish, afternoon drive to the Gulf coast.

  • Day 5

    Beach day, optional catamaran and snorkelling trip in the morning, seafood dinner on the sand.

  • Day 6

    Free morning; late-afternoon Thai cooking session the children can join, farewell dinner.

  • Day 7

    Transfer to Bangkok airport, fly home.

Where you stay

A riverside hotel in Bangkok rather than a tower in the shopping district, a river resort in Kanchanaburi where the Kwai runs past the rooms, and a proper beach resort on the Gulf. Properties we have used for years, chosen for family rooms and pools the children can actually use, not for lobby chandeliers.

Who it’s for

Families with children from about five upwards, and grandparents who want to come, the pace is gentle, the drives short, the food easy to navigate for vegetarians with our escort ordering. Not for couples wanting nightlife or shopping marathons; we spend one full day less in Bangkok than most itineraries precisely because of that.
