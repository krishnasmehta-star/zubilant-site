The Golden Triangle: Delhi, Agra & Jaipur

Five nights across Delhi, Agra and Jaipur in the cool months, built around one thing most tours fumble: standing in front of the Taj Mahal at sunrise, before the day arrives.

The journey

Most Golden Triangle itineraries show you the Taj Mahal at eleven in the morning, from the middle of a crowd, in the flattest light of the day. That is not seeing the Taj. That is queueing near it. The monument was built to be seen at dawn, when the marble takes the colour of the sky and the gates have only just opened, and any itinerary that does not put you there at that hour has quietly decided your convenience matters more than the reason you came.

So we build the whole trip around that one morning. Agra gets a full night before the visit, not a drive-in from Delhi, which means you are at the East Gate at first light with a guide who has done this several hundred times and knows exactly where to stand. Delhi gets both its cities, the Mughal north and Lutyens' south, with an old-Delhi walk through the spice market at a pace grandparents can manage. Jaipur gets Amber Fort early, the City Palace after, and an evening in the old walled city when the shops light up and the traffic thins.

This is the route we would recommend to anyone bringing family to India for the first time, and to Indian families who have somehow never done it. It is short, it is gentle, and every single stop earns its place. Nobody comes home from a properly run Golden Triangle wondering what the fuss was about.

Day by day

  • Day 1

    Arrive Delhi, easy afternoon, evening drive past India Gate and Rashtrapati Bhavan when the lights come on, welcome dinner. Nothing strenuous, on purpose.

  • Day 2

    Old Delhi in the morning, Jama Masjid and a rickshaw ride through Chandni Chowk, Humayun's Tomb in the soft late-afternoon light.

  • Day 3

    Drive to Agra on the expressway (about 3.5 hours), Agra Fort in the afternoon, early dinner and an early night. Tomorrow starts before dawn.

  • Day 4

    Taj Mahal at sunrise, back to the hotel for a proper breakfast, then the drive to Jaipur with a stop at Fatehpur Sikri, the abandoned Mughal capital.

  • Day 5

    Amber Fort in the morning before the heat, City Palace and Jantar Mantar after lunch, evening walk through the bazaars of the old Pink City.

  • Day 6

    Hawa Mahal photo stop at breakfast time, then the drive back to Delhi and onward connections, or a Jaipur airport departure for those flying direct.

Where you stay

Comfortable, well-run four-star properties in all three cities, chosen for location as much as comfort: in Agra you are minutes from the East Gate, because that is what makes the sunrise visit civilised rather than heroic. These are hotels we have used for years, with kitchens that can handle a Jain thali and a six-year-old's dinner with equal calm.

Who it’s for

Families, multi-generational groups and first-time visitors to North India. The pace is gentle, the drives are on good expressways, and there is a lift and a clean bathroom at every stop. Not for travellers who have done this route twice already and want the offbeat version; this is the classic, done properly, and it does not pretend otherwise.
