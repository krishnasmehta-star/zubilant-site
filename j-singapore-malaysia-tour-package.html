Singapore & Malaysia: The Easy First Passport Stamp

Six nights across Singapore, Kuala Lumpur and the Genting highlands, the classic first international trip, engineered so that nobody in the family, from seven to seventy-seven, has a hard day.

The journey

There is a reason Singapore has been the first stamp in Indian passports for forty years, and it is not the Merlion. It is that everything works. English everywhere, Indian food on every corner, pavements you could eat off, and a city built so a seventy-year-old and a seven-year-old enjoy the same afternoon. Most operators rush it in two nights to squeeze in more of Malaysia. We give it three, because the first international trip should feel unhurried, not like a checklist being run against the clock.

Our Singapore is Gardens by the Bay at dusk when the Supertrees light up, a morning on Sentosa where the grandparents ride the cable car while the children do the luge, and an evening in Little India that makes the family realise how far Indian food has travelled. Then the coach north to Kuala Lumpur, Petronas Towers from the ground and from the Skybridge, the Batu Caves for those who can manage the steps, and a night up in the cool air of Genting, where the funicular does the climbing for you. Families preferring sea to hills can take the Langkawi variant instead; ask us.

This is the journey we recommend when someone says "we have never been abroad and we want it to go well." Two countries, one language you already speak, and an escort who has done this run more times than he will admit.

Day by day

  • Day 1

    Arrive Singapore, transfer, evening stroll along Marina Bay, no schedule, deliberately; the first day abroad should be easy.

  • Day 2

    City morning (Merlion, Chinatown, Little India), Gardens by the Bay from late afternoon so you catch the Supertree light show.

  • Day 3

    Sentosa day, cable car, S.E.A. Aquarium, beach time; luge for the young and the young-ish; night safari optional.

  • Day 4

    Coach across the causeway to Kuala Lumpur (about 5 hours with a proper lunch stop); Petronas Towers lit up after dinner.

  • Day 5

    KL morning, Skybridge slot booked in advance, Batu Caves for those up to the 272 steps, others take the market instead; afternoon up to Genting by cable car.

  • Day 6

    Genting at leisure, cool air, the theme park for the children, the viewpoint walk for everyone else; farewell dinner.

  • Day 7

    Drive down to KL airport, fly home.

Where you stay

Centrally located hotels in Singapore and KL, close enough to walk out after dinner, which matters more than a star rating on a first trip. In Genting, a hilltop hotel where the entertainment is downstairs and nobody has to drive anywhere at night. All properties we have used for years, with connecting and ground-floor rooms arranged where families need them.

Who it’s for

First-time international travellers, multi-generational family groups, and anyone taking parents abroad for the first time, the whole journey is built so the oldest and youngest members have the same good week. Not for seasoned travellers wanting the undiscovered; this is the well-trodden path, walked properly, and we are not pretending otherwise.
