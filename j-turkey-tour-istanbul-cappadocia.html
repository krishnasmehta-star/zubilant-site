Turkey: Istanbul & Cappadocia

Seven nights across Istanbul and Cappadocia: Hagia Sophia and the Bosphorus done at the right hours, and two dawn chances at the balloons, because one is a gamble.

The journey

Here is what the balloon-ride photographs never tell you: the balloons do not fly every day. Wind cancels them, often, and most tours give you exactly one morning in Cappadocia, which means a coin-flip on the single image you came for. We give you three nights and two booked dawn slots, so if the first morning is cancelled you fly the second. It is still weather, and weather makes no promises, but two chances is roughly twice the odds, and we would rather build the itinerary around the truth than around the brochure.

Istanbul gets four nights, because it deserves them. Hagia Sophia and the Blue Mosque early, before the cruise-ship groups land; the Grand Bazaar with a local guide who knows which alleys sell to Istanbullus and which sell to tourists; and a Bosphorus crossing at golden hour, when the city does the thing it has done for fifteen centuries and makes you stop talking mid-sentence. The food alone justifies the trip, and dinners are included and chosen with intent, from meyhane mezes to a proper Cappadocian pottery kebab.

This is a journey for couples and for culture travellers, and the pace is honestly Moderate: Istanbul is a city of hills and cobbles, Cappadocia's valleys are uneven underfoot, and the balloon mornings start at 4:30. April to June and September to November are the months; summer is hot and crowded, and deep winter grounds too many balloons.

Day by day

  • Day 1

    Arrive Istanbul, transfer in, evening walk through Sultanahmet square with the minarets lit, dinner near the hotel. Easy by design.

  • Day 2

    Hagia Sophia at opening time, Blue Mosque and the Hippodrome, afternoon in the Basilica Cistern, evening free.

  • Day 3

    Topkapi Palace in the morning, Grand Bazaar and Spice Bazaar with a guide after lunch, golden-hour Bosphorus cruise, meyhane dinner.

  • Day 4

    Morning flight to Cappadocia, afternoon in Göreme Open-Air Museum among the painted cave churches, sunset at Red Valley viewpoint.

  • Day 5

    Balloon slot one at dawn, weather permitting, then Kaymaklı underground city and a pottery workshop in Avanos, early night.

  • Day 6

    Balloon slot two if needed, or a free dawn if you already flew, then a guided walk through Rose Valley and the fairy chimneys of Pasabag.

  • Day 7

    Fly back to Istanbul, afternoon in the Karaköy and Galata quarters, farewell dinner with the Bosphorus below.

  • Day 8

    Transfer to the airport and fly home.

Where you stay

In Istanbul, a well-run hotel in walking distance of Sultanahmet, because the early starts only work if the monuments are ten minutes away. In Cappadocia, a genuine cave hotel of the kind the region is famous for, rooms carved into the rock, terraces facing the balloon field. Properties we have used for years and would stay in ourselves.

Who it’s for

Couples and culture-first travellers who will trade two early mornings for the best hours in two of the world's great landscapes. Honestly Moderate: cobbles, hills, uneven valley paths and 4:30 alarms on balloon days. Not for travellers who need a lift to every room, cave hotels rarely have them, and not for anyone unwilling to accept that the balloons might not fly at all.
