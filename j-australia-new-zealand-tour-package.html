Australia & New Zealand: The Big Trip

Twelve nights from Sydney's harbour to the Great Barrier Reef to Queenstown and Milford Sound, the once-in-a-lifetime southern journey, done in their summer, at a pace that lets you actually be there.

The journey

Nobody should do Australia and New Zealand in eight nights, and plenty of operators will sell you exactly that. The flights alone are brutal, you are crossing the equator and most of a hemisphere, and a trip this far, this expensive, taken usually once, deserves to be built around being places rather than reaching them. So we take twelve nights, four flights fewer than the compressed versions, and we go between October and March, when it is their summer and our winter, which is the entire point.

Sydney gets three proper nights: the Opera House and the harbour by ferry rather than by coach window, the Blue Mountains for a day, and an evening at Darling Harbour with no bus to catch. Then north to Cairns for the reef, a full day on the outer Great Barrier Reef, where the coral is still the colour the documentaries promised, with glass-bottom and semi-submersible options for those who prefer to stay dry. Then across the Tasman to Queenstown, which may be the most beautifully situated town either country owns, for the lake, the Skyline gondola, and the day-long run to Milford Sound through Fiordland, waterfalls off thousand-metre cliffs, dolphins if the sound is in the mood.

Be honest with yourself about two things before booking. First, the price: this is a long-haul flagship and we would rather quote it truthfully than advertise a number that doubles by checkout. Second, the visa: Australia and New Zealand each require their own visitor visa for Indian passport holders, and processing can run four to eight weeks, book three months out, minimum. This lands hardest for retirees marking a milestone and families making the one big southern trip; it is built to be worth the distance.

Day by day

  • Day 1

    Arrive Sydney, transfer, harbour-side evening walk, deliberately empty after the long flight.

  • Day 2

    Sydney by ferry, Opera House, Circular Quay, Manly; Harbour Bridge from the water and the ground.

  • Day 3

    Blue Mountains day, Echo Point, the Three Sisters and Scenic World; back for a Darling Harbour dinner.

  • Day 4

    Fly to Cairns; afternoon at leisure on the Esplanade lagoon, tropical pace begins.

  • Day 5

    Full day on the outer Great Barrier Reef, snorkelling, semi-submersible, marine biologist talk on board.

  • Day 6

    Kuranda day, the Scenic Railway up, the Skyrail cableway back over the rainforest canopy; rest evening before the Tasman hop.

  • Day 7

    Fly to Queenstown via Auckland; evening arrival, lakefront dinner with the Remarkables turning pink.

  • Day 8

    Queenstown day, Skyline gondola, lake cruise on the vintage steamship, arrowtown gold-rush lanes in the afternoon.

  • Day 9

    Milford Sound day, the long, staggering road through Fiordland with photo stops, cruise beneath Mitre Peak, back by evening. The longest day of the trip, and the one you will describe most.

  • Day 10

    Free day in Queenstown, wine tour to Gibbston Valley, jet boat, or blissful nothing; escort on hand for all three.

  • Day 11

    Glenorchy morning at the head of the lake, farewell dinner in town.

  • Day 12

    Fly to Auckland; evening harbour stroll and last dinner.

  • Day 13

    Transfer to Auckland airport, fly home.

Where you stay

Four-star city hotels chosen for position: walking distance to Circular Quay in Sydney, the Esplanade in Cairns, and the lakefront in Queenstown, because on a trip this long the ability to step out after dinner is worth more than an extra star. Properties we have used for years, with twin and interconnecting configurations for family groups.

Who it’s for

Retirees marking a milestone, and families making the one big southern-hemisphere trip, the pace is deliberately humane, with a rest evening before every flight day. You need to be comfortable with three internal flights and one long coach day to Milford. Not for tight budgets or tight calendars: done honestly, this journey needs twelve nights and costs what it costs, and we would rather say so than compress it into something you regret.
