Egypt: Cairo & the Nile

Seven nights from the pyramids of Giza to a proper four-night Nile cruise, Aswan to Luxor, sailing the river the way Egypt has always been meant to be seen.

The journey

There are two ways to see Upper Egypt: by coach along a desert highway with a monument-a-day checklist, or by boat, the way every traveller from Herodotus onward actually did it. The coach version is cheaper and it shows. The temples of the Nile were built facing the river; arriving by water is not a romantic flourish, it is the correct angle. So the spine of this journey is a four-night cruise from Aswan downstream to Luxor, with the temples of Kom Ombo and Edfu taken as the boat reaches them and afternoons spent watching the riverbank go by from the sun deck with a glass of hibiscus tea.

Cairo gets the bookends. The pyramids and the Sphinx early in the morning, before the heat and the tour buses; the Egyptian Museum's Tutankhamun collection with a guide who is an Egyptologist by training, which in Egypt is the difference between a museum and a story. In Luxor, the Valley of the Kings and Karnak, which no photograph has ever adequately warned anyone about.

This lands hardest for culture-driven travellers and multi-generational families: the cruise makes Egypt gentle in a way land itineraries cannot, because the boat carries you while you rest. October to April is the season; an Egyptian summer is an experience we do not sell.

Day by day

  • Day 1

    Arrive Cairo, transfer in, evening at leisure with dinner at the hotel. Deliberately quiet.

  • Day 2

    Giza pyramids and the Sphinx at morning opening, lunch stop, Egyptian Museum with an Egyptologist guide in the afternoon.

  • Day 3

    Morning flight to Aswan, board the cruise boat, afternoon felucca sail around Elephantine Island, first night on the Nile.

  • Day 4

    Philae Temple in the morning, sail north through the afternoon, tea on the sun deck as the desert slides past.

  • Day 5

    Kom Ombo at sunrise, sail on to Edfu and its remarkably intact Temple of Horus by horse-drawn calèche, evening on board.

  • Day 6

    Sail into Luxor, afternoon at Karnak's great hypostyle hall, sound-and-light option in the evening for those who want it.

  • Day 7

    Valley of the Kings and the Temple of Hatshepsut early, disembark, afternoon flight back to Cairo, farewell dinner.

  • Day 8

    Transfer to the airport and fly home.

Where you stay

In Cairo, a comfortable hotel on the Nile-facing side of the city, close to the museum. On the river, a five-star cruise boat of the classic kind: wood-panelled dining room, small pool on the sun deck, cabins with picture windows at water level. We have used the same operators for years; on the Nile, the boat is half the holiday, and we choose accordingly.

Who it’s for

Culture-first travellers and multi-generational families, including grandparents: the cruise does the carrying, and site visits are taken early and unhurried. Gentle to Moderate is honest, because the Valley of the Kings involves heat, steps and sloped tomb corridors. Not for beach-holiday travellers, and not for anyone who needs every hour scheduled; the river insists on its own pace.
