Gulmarg in Winter: India's Ski Story

Five nights in Gulmarg between January and March, built around a proper beginners' ski school, the family ski holiday that usually means Europe, at a fraction of the cost and eight time zones closer.

The journey

Here is what the ski world knows and Indian travellers mostly don't: Gulmarg gets some of the deepest, driest snow in the Himalaya, and the Gondola's second phase reaches nearly 4,000 metres, higher than most lifts in the Alps. What Gulmarg has lacked is not snow but organisation, and that is a solvable problem. The difference between a frustrating first ski trip and a great one is entirely in the arrangements: pre-booked instructors, correctly fitted equipment, and someone who knows which mornings the Gondola queue is worth beating.

We build the week around learning. Three days of ski school on the beginners' slopes with instructors we have worked with season after season, genuine teaching, not a photograph on rental skis. Teenagers typically outpace their parents by day two, which is half the entertainment. Non-skiers are not an afterthought: sledging, snowshoe walks through the Khilanmarg meadows, and the Gondola ride itself, which needs no skill at all, only a window seat and a clear morning.

This lands hardest for families with teens making their first ski trip, and for anyone who assumed skiing was a foreign-holiday word. Fair warning where it is due: this is a mountain in winter, and weather, not the itinerary, has the final say.

Day by day

  • Day 1

    Arrive Srinagar, drive to Gulmarg (2 hrs, snow chains as needed). Equipment fitting in the afternoon so the morning starts on snow, not in a rental queue. Easy first evening by the bukhari.

  • Day 2

    Ski school day one, basics on the beginners' slopes, gentle and patient. Afternoon free for sledging and snowmen.

  • Day 3

    Ski school day two, linking turns, first small runs. Non-skiers take the snowshoe walk toward Khilanmarg.

  • Day 4

    Gondola day, Phase 1 to Kongdoori for everyone, Phase 2 to Apharwat viewpoint weather permitting. Afternoon rest; altitude respects no schedule.

  • Day 5

    Ski school day three, consolidating on fuller slopes, the day it clicks. Farewell dinner, kahwa and wazwan dishes.

  • Day 6

    Drive to Srinagar airport for departure (allow extra time in snow).

Where you stay

A warm, well-run Gulmarg hotel a short walk from the beginners' slopes, the walk matters more than the star rating when you are carrying skis. Bukhari-heated lounges, kitchens that understand hungry skiers, and staff we have worked with across many winters.

Who it’s for

Families with teenagers, groups of friends, and any adult who wants to learn to ski without a European price tag. Not for travellers who need certainty, snowfall closes roads and lifts, and flexibility is part of the deal. Not recommended for those with significant heart or respiratory conditions given the altitude, and toddlers will get more from this trip in a few years' time.
